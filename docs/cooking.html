Cooking

Family, friendship,
and generosity.

Long before "organic" or "slow food" became fashionable, the women in my family simply cooked the way they believed people should eat — with generosity and respect for those they loved. That is where this started.

How it started

My love for cooking started long before I ever held a knife in my own kitchen. It began with my family. I grew up surrounded by extraordinary women who expressed their love through food. My mother and my grandmother were remarkable cooks. Every day, our table was filled with homemade dishes prepared with care, patience, and good ingredients.

At twenty years old, life took me to England for a year. For a young French-speaking Swiss man raised on home cooking, it felt like entering a culinary desert. The contrast was brutal. Faced with what I jokingly call "the kitchen desolation," I had two choices: surrender to ready-made meals and slowly become depressed — or learn to cook.

I chose the second option. I called my mum.

Patiently, over the phone, she taught me simple recipes that even a student with limited means could prepare. Around the same time, I discovered that many of my friends were facing exactly the same challenge. None of us wanted to survive on mediocre food, so we transformed necessity into adventure.

We started organising cooking competitions. Each of us had to create the best possible dish using whatever ingredients we could find. There were no professional chefs, no sophisticated equipment, and certainly no Michelin inspectors. There was only creativity, laughter, a healthy sense of competition, and the shared belief that life was simply too short to eat badly. Those evenings shaped the cook I would become.

The tiramisu that started a legend

During that period, I also met an Italian girlfriend who introduced me to what remains, in my completely unbiased opinion, the best tiramisu in the world.

Many friends have tried to challenge that title over the years. They have brought their grandmothers' recipes, secret techniques, and carefully guarded traditions. Yet somehow, mine always ended up being the crowd favourite.

Welcome to my table

Today, cooking remains one of the great pleasures of my life. I cook for my family almost every day. At least twice a month, I invite friends to our home to share good food, long conversations, plenty of laughter, and, naturally, a few exceptional bottles of wine.

Because for me, cooking has never been only about feeding people. It is about creating moments. In a world that moves faster every day, preparing a meal from scratch can almost feel like a small act of rebellion. It requires slowing down, paying attention, choosing quality over convenience, and gathering people around the same table.

My cooking style is generous, colourful, and curious. I love spices and I enjoy exploring recipes from all over the world. French classics sit comfortably alongside Italian comfort food, Indian flavours, Swiss specialities, and discoveries gathered through travel, friendships, and simple experimentation.

Good food reminds us that generosity is contagious,
that traditions are meant to be shared,
and that some of life's greatest memories are created
between the first bite and the final glass.
